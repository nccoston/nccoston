How it's measured

The picture is fixed. Every soundtrack has to live inside the English actor's mouth: the same windows, and the lips closing on the same frames. Two things are checked per line:

  • Timing. Syllables are converted to expected speaking time using average spontaneous rates: English about 6.2 syllables/sec, Spanish about 7.8 (Pellegrino, Coupé & Marsico, 2011). Spanish counting applies synalepha, so vowels merge across word boundaries the way they do when spoken. A ratio of 1.00 means the line fills the window exactly.
  • Lip closures. Bilabials, where the lips visibly shut (b, p, m, and Spanish v), are placed along the line. Blue ticks are the picture's closures and amber ticks are the dub's. A tick scores when it has a partner within 12% of the line. Closures in the wrong place count against you, because a shut mouth on screen under an open vowel is the error audiences notice.

These are heuristics over spelling, not phoneme alignment. They're built to be roughly right and easy to read. The sync score is 55% timing and 45% lips. When you play a scene, the page also measures the synthetic voice's real durations. With Fit to picture on, the voice's rate is set per line to fill the window, and the rate needed is reported. A literal line that only fits at 1.4× is a line an actor can't deliver.
